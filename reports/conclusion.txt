The model performs better at identifying customers who do not churn (0), with high precision and moderate recall. For churned customers (1), recall is relatively strong, meaning the model catches most at-risk customers, but precision is lower, so it also produces a noticeable number of false positives.The main weakness is the low precision for class 1, meaning the model produces many false positives when predicting churn. In practice, this can lead to unnecessary retention efforts for customers who would not have churned.From a feature‑importance perspective, the model relies most heavily on contract type (especially month‑to‑month), monthly charges, internet service type (fiber optic vs DSL), total charges, customer tenure, payment method (notably electronic check), and the presence of add‑on services such as online security and tech support. In practice, this means the model tends to flag as high risk those customers who are relatively new, pay higher monthly fees, and are on short‑term contracts, while customers with long tenure, multi‑year contracts, and additional support/security services are modeled as less likely to churn—even though the low precision for class 1 implies that some of these flagged high‑risk customers would actually stay without intervention.
